Day 10 — Udaipur: Art, Heritage and Markets

Day 10, Monday 19 October

Udaipur: palace rooms, painters and market lanes

The last full day, with art at its centre.

In this chapter

  2. Shilpgram: buying from the people who make it
  3. Meeting miniature-painting artists

Our final full day in Rajasthan began with City Palace. After visiting so many forts and palaces, one might expect them to start blending together. They did not. Each region has its own history and visual language, and Udaipur’s City Palace offered another rich collection of courtyards, paintings, decorative surfaces and views over the lake.

City Palace and a change of hotel

Udaipur’s City Palace grew over centuries under the rulers of Mewar. Its interconnected buildings, balconies, courtyards and decorative rooms make it a place to explore slowly. Mor Chowk, known for its peacock mosaics, is particularly famous. I was drawn to the craftsmanship throughout — the kind of work that makes you wonder how much patience and skill went into every surface.

After the visit, we shifted from Udaigarh to Panna Vilas and took some rest. By this point in the trip, even the most enthusiastic traveller appreciates a pause. We still had one more outing planned, however, and it was particularly close to my interests.

Shilpgram: buying from the people who make it

We hired an auto-rickshaw and went to Shilpgram, the crafts village associated with traditional rural arts and crafts. Here I purchased sarees and dress materials directly from weavers and artisans. That direct interaction mattered to me. It is one thing to admire a finished object in a shop; it is another to meet the people whose hands and knowledge have produced it.

We continued to Hathipole, Bapu Bazaar and Mochiwada Bazaar, exploring the markets and buying clothes and gifts to take home. Rajasthan had tempted me with crafts from the beginning, and our last full day naturally included shopping. It felt like a fitting end to a journey that had started with a wish to see where so many beautiful handmade things come from.

Meeting miniature-painting artists

Another especially memorable Udaipur experience was visiting an art gallery and meeting artists who work in the miniature-painting tradition. I learned about their processes, including how colours are prepared and how brushes are made. We exchanged profiles, and I purchased a painting. I had taken so many photographs of frescoes, carved details, painted walls and architectural patterns during the journey; now I had also spoken with artists about the making of art itself.

I am deliberately not describing the subject of the painting I bought, because that detail is not necessary to tell the story. What mattered was the conversation and the chance to learn directly from people carrying a traditional practice forward.

Why Udaipur became my favourite

I loved many places in Rajasthan, but Udaipur stood out for the overall experience. Its lakes, royal architecture, markets and art were beautiful, but I also appreciated its relative cleanliness and the wider roads we encountered. On much of our journey I had been disappointed to see discarded plastic covers, paper and other rubbish along roadsides. That was my observation as a traveller, not a claim that every place in Rajasthan is the same. Still, for a state that preserves so much magnificent heritage, I wished the everyday surroundings received equal care.

Another observation stayed with me from our drives through rural areas. In several villages we passed, I saw very few women out on the roads or in public spaces, whereas Jaipur, Jodhpur and Udaipur felt quite different. A view from a passing car cannot explain how people live, and I would not pretend it can. But the contrast made me curious about the many different social worlds within one state.

Udaipur gave me beauty, comfort, art and a sense of ease. That combination made it my favourite destination of the journey.
