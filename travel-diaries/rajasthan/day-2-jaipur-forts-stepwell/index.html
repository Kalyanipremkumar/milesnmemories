Part of My Rajasthan Diaries · Day 2 of 11

Day 2, Sunday 11 October

Jaipur: forts and a childhood memory

Amer, Jaigarh, Nahargarh and the stepwell that brought back Sirivennela.

Our second day in Jaipur was devoted to its hill forts and a stepwell that had a special place in my imagination. The forts around Jaipur are more than scenic viewpoints; together they help explain how royal residences, military defence and the surrounding landscape once fitted into a single system.

Amer: grandeur in layers

Amer Fort rises above the town of Amer, its pale walls and gateways following the contours of the hills. Its courtyards lead from public spaces into increasingly private royal areas. Ganesh Pol, with its elaborate decoration, is among the features that reward a closer look. Sheesh Mahal, associated with intricate mirror work, shows how reflective surfaces can transform a room with light. I found myself thinking not only about the kings and queens who lived here, but about the artists and craftspeople who spent so much time on each detail.

Amer is also a reminder that Rajasthan’s architecture is never just one style. Rajput traditions, Mughal influences, practical responses to heat and the demands of court life all appear in different parts of the complex. For anyone who loves visual detail, it is worth slowing down rather than hurrying from one photo spot to another.

Jaigarh and Nahargarh: another view of Jaipur

Jaigarh, positioned above Amer, was associated with the defence of the region. Its ramparts, commanding views and the famous Jaivana cannon tell a story quite different from the decorated chambers below. A fort built for protection has its own kind of beauty: thick walls, strategic positions and the feeling of the landscape opening out around you.

Nahargarh, overlooking Jaipur, completed our circuit. From the hills, the planned city becomes easier to appreciate as a whole. It is one thing to walk through a street lined with terracotta buildings and quite another to look down towards the city from above. Each fort had a different personality, and together they made the day memorable.

Panna Meena ka Kund: a memory from childhood

The place that carried a particularly personal meaning for me was Panna Meena ka Kund, the old stepwell near Amer. As a child, I had watched the Telugu film Sirivennela and remembered a song featuring a beautiful stepwell. That image stayed with me for years. I wanted, someday, to stand beside a place like it and take photographs.

Panna Meena ka Kund is not the actual stepwell shown in the film. But its geometry — repeated flights of steps descending in carefully arranged patterns — brought that old memory back. Stepwells were practical water structures, especially important in regions where storing and accessing water mattered enormously. Yet their symmetry also makes them visually captivating. Looking at those steps, I was seeing both an example of traditional engineering and a small piece of my own childhood imagination.

Travelling often does this: a place becomes meaningful not only because of its history, but because of something we have carried within us for years. That was my connection to this stepwell. After a day of impressive forts, it was a quieter, more personal memory to take away.
