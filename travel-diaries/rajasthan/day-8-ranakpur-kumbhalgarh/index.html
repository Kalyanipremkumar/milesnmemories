    Day 8 — Ranakpur and Kumbhalgarh

    Day 8, Saturday 17 October

    Ranakpur and Kumbhalgarh: stone, faith and memory

    A marble temple and a hill fort connected to a childhood hero.

    In this chapter

    1. Ranakpur: marble that seems almost weightless
    2. Kumbhalgarh: the landscape of Maharana Pratap

    We travelled through the Aravalli landscape towards Ranakpur and Kumbhalgarh. By now, I had seen many kinds of Rajasthani architecture, yet these two places offered another contrast: a temple celebrated for its intricacy and a fort celebrated for its immense defensive setting.

    Ranakpur: marble that seems almost weightless

    Ranakpur Jain Temple is famous for its richly carved marble and its many pillars — traditionally described as 1,444, with intricate variations in their decoration. The temple complex is associated with the fifteenth century and is dedicated primarily to Adinatha, the first Jain Tirthankara. What fascinated me was the discipline behind the work: so much repetition, yet so much variation. Stone is a hard material, but in skilled hands it can seem delicate and light.

    For someone who loves painting and pattern, Ranakpur offered a different lesson. Lines that I might draw with a pen or brush had been translated into carved stone. The details invite patience; there is far more to see than a quick glance can absorb.

    Kumbhalgarh: the landscape of Maharana Pratap

    Kumbhalgarh Fort is part of the historic hill-fort tradition of Rajasthan and is known for its extensive walls, often described as stretching for about 36 kilometres. It is also traditionally recognised as the birthplace of Maharana Pratap. For me, that association was meaningful. I had grown up hearing stories of his determination and of Chetak, the horse whose loyalty became legendary in popular retellings.

    It is one thing to hear those stories as a child, and another to travel through the region connected to them. The hills and fortifications made the scale of the landscape easier to imagine. I do not want to invent a dramatic moment of emotion that I did not record at the time; it is enough to say that this was a place linked to a figure I had admired since childhood.

    We stayed overnight at Kumbhalgarh Junagarh Resort. The following morning would take us towards Nathdwara, Eklingji and finally Udaipur.
