    Day 5 — Jaisalmer: The Golden City

    Day 5, Wednesday 14 October

    Jaisalmer: sunset among the cenotaphs

    From Bikaner to the Golden City, arriving just in time for evening light.

    We travelled onward to Jaisalmer, a city whose identity is inseparable from the golden-yellow sandstone used in its buildings. As the sun changes position, the stone seems to change colour too. That is part of the reason Jaisalmer is so often called the Golden City. Its location also recalls the caravan routes and trade networks that once connected desert settlements with distant markets.

    Bada Bagh at six

    We reached Bada Bagh at around 6 p.m. and watched the sunset. The site is known for its royal cenotaphs — memorial structures whose domes and columns make a striking pattern against the desert landscape. The low evening light suited the stone beautifully. After a long road journey, it was a memorable way to enter the Jaisalmer part of our trip: not through a busy market or a hotel lobby, but through a quiet landscape of memorial architecture and sunset.

    We checked into Heritage Inn afterwards. Not every hotel on a long journey becomes a favourite, and this one was not among our best stays. I think it is worth saying so. Travel diaries should include the ordinary disappointments as well as the grand sights; otherwise every day begins to sound suspiciously perfect.

    Jaisalmer still had much more waiting for us. The following day would bring carved havelis, a living fort, a reunion with a friend and, finally, the desert itself.
