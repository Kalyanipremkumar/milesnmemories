This is not a catalogue of monuments or a list of places to tick off. It is the story of a long-awaited journey, seen through my interest in history, architecture, paintings, crafts, and the everyday details that make travel personal. I have included background about the places we visited, but the impressions, preferences and occasional disappointments are my own. When I repeat a particular story told by a guide, I identify it as such.

The journey before the journey

Rajasthan had been on my mind for more than ten years. We had travelled widely in India, particularly to sacred places: together, we had visited many important pilgrimage sites across India. Pilgrimage taught us to travel with patience and curiosity, but my Rajasthan wish was also about something else: forts, museums, frescoes, carved wood, miniature paintings and the lives behind them.

Whenever I admired a beautifully carved wooden object at an exhibition or in a shop, I would ask where it came from. So often the answer was Jaipur, or somewhere in Rajasthan. Every such conversation added another reason to go. I wanted to see the crafts at their source, meet the people making them and understand the traditions behind the objects.

And then there was Maharana Pratap. As a child, I listened to my teacher narrate stories about his courage and about Chetak, his loyal horse. Those stories stayed with me. Long before I saw Rajasthan on a map, it existed in my imagination as a place of extraordinary courage and character.

I finally made this journey with my husband. He is not simply the person who travelled beside me. He is my favourite travelling companion. We had eleven days to explore, to stop for pictures, to disagree occasionally about how much more we could fit into a day, and to bring home memories that would last far longer than the holiday.
